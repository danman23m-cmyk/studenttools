How to use this GPA calculator

  1. Add one row for each course.
  2. Select the letter grade you expect or earned.
  3. Enter the course's credit hours.
  4. Select Calculate GPA to see your credit-weighted GPA.

How is GPA calculated?

A GPA is calculated by converting each letter grade to grade points, multiplying those points by the course credits, adding the quality points, and dividing by the total credits.

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credits
Quality points = Grade points × Credit hours

GPA calculation example

Suppose you take three courses: an A in 3 credits, a B+ in 3 credits, and an A- in 4 credits. Using the scale shown below, the calculation is:

(4.0 × 3 + 3.3 × 3 + 3.7 × 4) ÷ 10 = 3.67 GPA

Common 4.0 GPA scale

This is a common U.S. reference scale. Your school may use different grade-point values, A+ rules, rounding, or plus/minus policies, so check your institution's official grading policy for an official GPA.

Credit-weighted vs. simple GPA average

This calculator weights courses by credit hours. A 4-credit course has twice the influence of a 2-credit course. Simply averaging the GPA values of each course can give a different result when courses have different credit hours.

Is this an official GPA?

No. This tool provides an estimate based on the displayed 4.0 reference scale. Colleges and universities can use their own rules for plus/minus grades, A+ values, repeated courses, pass/fail courses, exclusions, weighting, and rounding. Use your transcript or school's official GPA calculation when you need an official number.

Frequently asked questions

What is a GPA?

GPA stands for grade point average. It summarizes course performance as a number on a grading scale such as 4.0.

How many courses can I add?

You can add as many course rows as you need. Empty rows are ignored when the calculator runs.

Does this GPA calculator use credits?

Yes. Course grade points are weighted by the credit hours you enter, which is useful for estimating a college-style semester GPA.

Can I calculate cumulative GPA?

Yes. Enter your previous cumulative GPA and previous completed credits in the optional section, then add the new courses. The calculator combines the previous quality points with the new course quality points.

Why might my school's GPA be different?

Schools can use different grade-point scales and policies. For example, an A+ may have a different value, some schools omit plus/minus grades, and repeated or pass/fail courses may be treated differently.
